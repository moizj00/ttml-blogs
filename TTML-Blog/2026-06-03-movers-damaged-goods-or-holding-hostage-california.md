# The Movers Broke Your Stuff — or Won't Unload It Until You Pay More. Your Options.

Two ugly versions of the same day. In the first, the boxes arrive and half of what's inside is broken. In the second, the truck is at the curb, your whole life is inside it, and the driver says the price just went up — nothing comes off until you pay.

Both feel like you're powerless. In California, you have more standing than they're counting on.

## They can't hold your belongings hostage

Start with the second one, because it feels like extortion. A mover demanding a sudden, inflated payment before they'll release your goods is on very thin ice. Holding a shipment hostage over a disputed or padded bill is the kind of practice regulators treat as a serious violation, not a negotiating tactic.

You don't have to win that fight on the sidewalk. Pay what you can document you actually agreed to if you must, get your things inside, and contest the rest in writing — from a position of strength, with your belongings already off the truck.

## Damage is a claim, and you have the right to file it

When movers break or lose your things, you're entitled to file a claim, and they're obligated to process it. What you can recover turns on the valuation coverage you picked when you booked — the gap between bare per-pound coverage and full replacement value is the fine print most people never read until something shatters.

Photograph everything: the damage, the boxes, the inventory sheet the crew had you sign. That inventory and the bill of lading are the documents the whole dispute turns on.

## The letter that gets it taken seriously

Movers field angry phone calls all day. A written demand is a different animal. It lays out what was damaged or overcharged, what you're owed, and a deadline — and it signals that you know this can go to a regulator and a court, not just a customer-service queue.

(Moving disputes follow different rules depending on whether the move crossed state lines or stayed inside California, and coverage terms vary by contract. Confirm your situation with a licensed attorney before relying on any of this.)
