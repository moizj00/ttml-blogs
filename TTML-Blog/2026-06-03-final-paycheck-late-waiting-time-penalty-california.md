# Your Final Paycheck Is Late. California Pays You for the Wait.

Your last paycheck isn't due "next payday." When a California job ends, the final check comes fast — and if your employer drags it out, the law makes them pay you for every day you wait.

## The deadlines, plainly

California sets hard timing on a final paycheck (Labor Code § 201 and § 202):

- **Fired or laid off?** You're owed everything — final wages plus accrued, unused vacation — immediately, on your last day.
- **Quit with at least 72 hours' notice?** It's due on your last day.
- **Quit without notice?** They have 72 hours to get it to you.

"We run payroll on the 15th" is not an exception. The end of a job starts its own clock.

## The part employers hope you don't know

Miss those deadlines and California adds a penalty with teeth. Under Labor Code § 203, an employer who willfully fails to pay on time owes you your average daily wage for every day the check is late, up to 30 days.

Do the math. On a $200-a-day job, a check that's two weeks late can carry a penalty in the hundreds. A full 30-day delay can mean an extra month's pay on top of what you were already owed. The penalty is built to sting, because the deadline is meant to be taken seriously.

## Put it in writing first

Before anything escalates, a letter often does the job. Plenty of late checks are sloppiness rather than malice, and a clear written demand — citing the deadline and the running penalty — gets attention from whoever signs off on payroll.

State what you're owed, the date your job ended, the deadline they missed, and the daily penalty now running. Keep it factual. Send it a way you can prove it landed.

(Wage law has conditions and exceptions, and "willful" has a specific meaning here. Confirm your situation with a licensed attorney or the California Labor Commissioner before relying on any of this.)
