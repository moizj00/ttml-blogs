If someone is selling counterfeit versions of your product in California, you can report them to the California Attorney General through the Department of Justice's online complaint form at oag.ca.gov. The AG's office has authority to investigate and prosecute counterfeit goods under Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17500 (false advertising), Cal. Penal Code § 350 (sale of counterfeit goods), and the state's Unfair Competition Law (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200). Filing takes about 15 minutes and costs nothing.

## What Evidence Should I Include in My Complaint?

The stronger your evidence, the more likely the AG's office will investigate. Include:

- **Proof of your authentic product** — photos, trademark registration numbers, product catalogs, or invoices showing you're the legitimate source.
- **Evidence of the counterfeit** — screenshots of the fake listings, photos of counterfeit products (if you purchased one), URLs, seller names, and dates.
- **Consumer harm** — any complaints you've received from customers who accidentally bought the fake, reviews mentioning quality problems, or safety concerns.
- **Your contact information** — the AG's office may follow up for additional details.

The complaint form asks for the business name, address (if known), and a description of the deceptive practice. Be specific and factual — exaggeration weakens your credibility.

## Will the Attorney General Actually Investigate?

The AG's office receives thousands of complaints and prioritizes cases involving widespread consumer harm, safety risks, or organized counterfeiting operations. A single complaint about one seller may not trigger an investigation on its own — but it creates a record. If multiple people report the same seller, the AG is much more likely to act.

For faster results on individual sellers, consider pairing the AG complaint with a direct [cease and desist letter](/blog/2026-05-13-cease-and-desist-letters-ecommerce-sellers-counterfeit-goods) sent to the counterfeiter and a takedown request to the platform.

## What Other Agencies Can I Report Counterfeit Sellers To?

Beyond the California AG, you have several options:

**Federal Trade Commission (FTC)** — ReportFraud.ftc.gov handles interstate counterfeit operations.

**U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)** — If counterfeit goods are being imported, CBP can seize shipments at the border. You can record your trademark with CBP for proactive enforcement.

**The National Intellectual Property Rights Coordination Center (IPR Center)** — Run by ICE/HSI, this handles large-scale counterfeiting investigations.

**Platform-specific reporting** — Amazon Brand Registry, eBay's VeRO program, and Etsy's IP infringement form let you report counterfeit listings directly.

## What's the Most Effective Strategy?

The AG complaint is part of a multi-pronged approach, not a standalone solution. The most effective strategy for most California sellers is: (1) send a cease and desist letter to the counterfeiter, (2) file platform takedown requests for immediate listing removal, (3) file the AG complaint to create a government record, and (4) register your trademark with CBP if you're dealing with imported fakes.

The cease and desist letter handles the immediate problem. The government complaints build a paper trail that strengthens any future enforcement action.
