To remove counterfeits of your product on Walmart Marketplace or TikTok Shop, a California seller files an intellectual property complaint through each platform's brand-protection or IP reporting system, backs it with proof of your trademark or copyright, and escalates with a cease and desist letter or lawsuit if the seller persists. Your underlying rights come from the federal Lanham Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1114 and § 1125, federal copyright law, and California's Unfair Competition Law, Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200. The mechanics differ by platform, but the strategy is the same: report fast, document everything, and escalate when needed.

**Short answer:** Use each platform's IP complaint form to get fakes taken down quickly, then send an attorney letter to stop repeat offenders and pursue damages. Registering your trademark and copyright first makes every takedown stronger.

## Why have counterfeits spread beyond Amazon?

For years, brand owners focused their anti-counterfeiting energy on Amazon because that's where the volume was. But as Walmart Marketplace opened to third-party sellers and TikTok Shop turned viral videos into instant storefronts, counterfeiters followed the traffic. These newer marketplaces onboard sellers quickly, which is great for legitimate businesses and also convenient for bad actors.

The result is that a California brand can find knockoffs of its product appearing simultaneously across several platforms, sometimes from the same overseas seller using different storefront names. Fighting counterfeits in 2026 means thinking in terms of a multi-platform campaign, not a single takedown.

## How do you remove a counterfeit on Walmart Marketplace?

Walmart operates a Brand Portal and an IP protection program for rights holders. The first step is to enroll and verify your brand, which generally requires a registered trademark. Once verified, you can submit IP complaints identifying the infringing listings, the type of violation (trademark, copyright, or patent), and the basis for your claim.

Walmart reviews these reports and can remove listings or suspend sellers who repeatedly infringe. As with any marketplace, the quality of your complaint matters: cite your registration number, point to the specific listing URLs, and explain clearly why the product is counterfeit or infringing rather than simply a competitor you dislike. Vague or overbroad complaints get rejected and can even expose you to counterclaims if you target legitimate resellers.

## How do you remove a counterfeit on TikTok Shop?

TikTok Shop has its own intellectual property protection process and an IP complaint portal where rights holders submit takedown requests. Because TikTok Shop blends content and commerce, infringement can show up in two places — the product listings themselves and the videos promoting them. You may need to report both the listing and the associated content.

A TikTok Shop IP complaint typically asks you to identify your right (trademark or copyright), provide proof such as a registration certificate, and specify the infringing products or content. TikTok can remove the listings, take down videos, and penalize repeat-infringing sellers. The platform moves quickly on well-documented complaints, so getting your evidence organized before you file speeds everything up.

## What evidence do you need before filing on any platform?

Every platform's process runs on proof, so assemble a small evidence file before you start. At minimum: your trademark registration certificate or copyright registration; clear screenshots of the infringing listings with their URLs and seller names; and a short, factual explanation of why the product is counterfeit — identical branding, copied photos, your logo on a product you never made.

If you can, buy a sample of the counterfeit to confirm it's actually fake and to document the discrepancies (poor quality, missing authentication features, fake packaging). That test buy can become powerful evidence later if you escalate to a letter or lawsuit. Keep the receipts and the item.

## When should you send a cease and desist instead of just reporting?

Platform takedowns solve the immediate symptom — the live listing disappears — but they don't always stop the seller, who can relist under a new name. A cease and desist letter targets the seller directly, demands they stop manufacturing and selling the knockoff, and warns of legal consequences. An attorney-signed letter signals you're prepared to pursue an injunction and damages under the Lanham Act and Section 17200.

Letters are most useful when you can identify the seller, when the same party keeps relisting, or when the counterfeiting is large enough to justify a damages claim. ## How do you fight counterfeits across multiple platforms at once?

Treat it as one campaign. Register your trademark and copyright so you qualify for every platform's brand-protection program. Enroll in each marketplace's portal — Walmart Brand Portal, TikTok Shop IP protection, Amazon Brand Registry — so you have standing reporting access. Then monitor consistently, because counterfeiters resurface. Many California sellers set a weekly routine: search their brand name on each marketplace, screenshot anything suspicious, and file complaints in a batch.

When the same overseas seller pops up everywhere, a single attorney letter referencing all the platforms and listings can be more efficient than chasing each one separately. And recording your trademark with U.S. Customs and Border Protection can stop counterfeit shipments before they ever reach a marketplace.

## What about counterfeiters you can't identify or reach?

Many counterfeiters hide behind shell storefronts and overseas addresses. When you can't reach the seller, platform enforcement and customs recordation become your main tools, because they work without needing to serve the infringer. In larger cases, brand owners sometimes pursue litigation that targets groups of anonymous sellers and freezes their marketplace payment accounts — an aggressive option usually reserved for serious, high-volume counterfeiting and best evaluated with counsel.

## The bottom line for California sellers

## How do Walmart and TikTok Shop differ from Amazon's process?

If you've fought counterfeits on Amazon, the muscle memory transfers, but the details differ. Amazon's Brand Registry is mature and tightly integrated with reporting tools and project-based takedowns. Walmart's Brand Portal is newer and more streamlined, with fewer bells and whistles but a clear IP complaint path once you're verified. TikTok Shop is the most content-driven of the three, so you're often policing short-form videos as much as product listings, and the pace of viral selling means a knockoff can rack up sales fast before you catch it.

The practical takeaway is that you can't simply copy your Amazon playbook line for line. Learn each platform's specific complaint form and evidence requirements, and budget time for the newer marketplaces to move differently than Amazon does. What stays constant across all three is the foundation: a registered trademark and clean, well-documented complaints.

## What mistakes get California sellers in trouble?

The biggest error is filing aggressive complaints against legitimate competitors or authorized resellers. Marketplaces penalize abusive reporting, and a seller you wrongly accuse of counterfeiting may have valid first-sale or gray-market defenses — and a counterclaim. Report only genuine counterfeits, and make sure your evidence supports the "counterfeit" label rather than just "a competitor I don't like."

A second mistake is treating takedowns as one-and-done. Counterfeiters relist. Build a recurring monitoring habit, keep your evidence organized, and escalate persistent offenders to an attorney letter rather than filing the same platform complaint over and over. Consistency, not intensity, is what actually protects your sales.

## When is litigation worth it?

For most California small sellers, platform enforcement plus attorney letters handle the problem. Litigation enters the picture when a counterfeiter is causing serious, quantifiable damage, when the same party keeps relisting across platforms, or when you need a court order to freeze accounts or stop imports. Because lawsuits are expensive and slow, they're best reserved for the cases where the recovery clearly justifies the cost — a judgment an attorney can help you make.

Counterfeits on Walmart Marketplace and TikTok Shop are removable, but the work is ongoing. Register your IP, enroll in every platform's brand-protection program, document infringements carefully, report fast, and escalate to attorney letters for repeat offenders. The sellers who treat anti-counterfeiting as a routine — not a one-time fire drill — keep the fakes from eating their sales.
