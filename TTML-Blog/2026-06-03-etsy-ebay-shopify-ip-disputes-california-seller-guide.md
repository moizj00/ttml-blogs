If you're a California ecommerce seller dealing with copycats, the enforcement process varies significantly by platform. Amazon gets most of the attention (we covered it in our [Amazon counterfeiting guide](/blog/2026-05-27-complete-guide-protecting-brand-counterfeits-amazon-california)), but Etsy, eBay, and Shopify each have their own IP complaint systems with different rules, timelines, and outcomes. Knowing how each platform works — and when platform-level enforcement isn't enough — is the difference between chasing takedowns endlessly and actually stopping the infringer.

This guide covers each platform's IP dispute process, typical response times, common pitfalls, and when to escalate from a platform complaint to an attorney-signed cease and desist letter.

## Etsy IP Disputes: How the Process Works

Etsy handles IP complaints through its Intellectual Property Policy and an online reporting form at etsy.com/legal/ip.

**What you can report:** Copyright infringement (stolen photos, descriptions, designs), trademark infringement (use of your brand name or logo), and counterfeit goods (products falsely bearing your trademark).

**How to file:** Submit the form with your contact information, identification of your IP right (copyright registration number, trademark registration number, or description of common-law rights), the URL of each infringing listing, and the required DMCA or trademark statements.

**Typical timeline:** Etsy usually reviews and acts on complaints within 1–3 business days. Infringing listings are removed, and the seller receives a strike. Three strikes can result in permanent shop suspension.

**What happens if the seller disputes:** For copyright claims, the seller can file a DMCA counter-notice. Etsy restores the listing after 10–14 business days unless you file a federal lawsuit and notify Etsy. For trademark claims, the seller can respond to Etsy explaining why their listing doesn't infringe — Etsy then makes a judgment call.

**Common Etsy pitfalls:**

Etsy's system favors specificity. Reporting that "their whole shop copies my style" won't work — you need to identify specific listings and specific IP rights. Additionally, Etsy distinguishes between "inspired by" designs and actual copies. If a seller creates a similar-looking product using their own photos and descriptions, Etsy may not consider it infringement unless you have a registered trademark or can show trade dress infringement.

## eBay IP Disputes: The VeRO Program

eBay's primary IP enforcement tool is the Verified Rights Owner (VeRO) program. It's more structured than Etsy's system — and more powerful once you're enrolled.

**Enrollment:** You must register as a VeRO member before you can report listings. Submit the enrollment form at pages.ebay.com/seller-center/listing-and-marketing/verified-rights-owner-program.html with proof of your IP rights. Approval takes 1–5 business days.

**What you can report:** Copyright infringement, trademark infringement, counterfeit goods, and trade dress infringement. VeRO covers a broader range of IP claims than most platform programs.

**How to file:** Once enrolled, use the VeRO reporting portal to submit takedown requests. You'll need the item numbers (eBay listing IDs) and the specific IP right being infringed.

**Typical timeline:** eBay removes reported listings within 24–48 hours of receiving a valid VeRO report. The seller receives a notice explaining the removal and the rights owner's contact information.

**What happens if the seller disputes:** The seller can contact you directly (eBay shares your contact information as part of VeRO) or appeal to eBay. If the dispute can't be resolved, the matter goes to court.

**VeRO advantages over other platforms:** eBay's VeRO system shares your contact information with the alleged infringer, which sounds alarming but actually helps — it often leads to direct resolution. VeRO also has a more established track record of supporting brand owners and permanently suspending repeat infringers.

**Common eBay pitfalls:** The enrollment process takes time, so don't wait until you have an active infringement problem to sign up. Also, VeRO reports that don't clearly identify the IP right being infringed are rejected — be specific about whether it's a copyright, trademark, or other right.

## Shopify IP Disputes: Going After Independent Stores

Shopify is fundamentally different from Etsy and eBay because it hosts independent stores rather than operating a marketplace. This means:

- There's no centralized search where customers might stumble across a copycat listing alongside yours
- Each Shopify store is an independent website, so you're dealing with the store owner directly
- Shopify acts as the hosting provider, not the marketplace operator

**How to file:** Send a DMCA takedown notice to Shopify's legal team at legal-dmca@shopify.com, or use their online IP complaint form. Your notice must meet all six DMCA requirements under 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3).

**Typical timeline:** Shopify forwards your notice to the store owner. If the store owner doesn't respond or file a counter-notice within 10 business days, Shopify removes the content. If they file a counter-notice, you have 10–14 business days to file a federal lawsuit or the content is restored.

**What Shopify will and won't do:** Shopify will remove specific infringing content (product photos, listing text) but generally won't shut down an entire store based on a single complaint. For trademark complaints involving counterfeit goods, Shopify may take stronger action — including suspending the store — especially if the infringement is blatant.

**Common Shopify pitfalls:** Because Shopify stores are independent websites, the store owner often has more leverage than a marketplace seller. They can file counter-notices more aggressively, and Shopify's DMCA process follows the strict statutory timeline — there's less discretion than Etsy or eBay exercise. This makes the cease and desist letter especially important for Shopify disputes.

## When Platform Enforcement Isn't Enough

Platform takedowns solve the immediate problem — the infringing listing comes down. But they don't solve the underlying problem: the infringer is still out there and can re-list, move to another platform, or set up a new account.

You should escalate to a cease and desist letter when:

- The same infringer re-lists after a takedown
- The infringer is operating across multiple platforms
- The infringement involves an entire product line, not just one listing
- You want to establish willfulness for increased damages in a potential lawsuit
- The platform's dispute resolution favors the infringer (common with Shopify counter-notices)

A cease and desist letter from an attorney puts the infringer on personal legal notice — not just a platform notification. It creates a legal record that the infringement was knowing and willful, which can triple your damages under the Lanham Act (15 U.S.C. § 1117) or unlock maximum statutory damages under the Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. § 504(c)).

## Platform Comparison: Response Times and Effectiveness

Across the three platforms, here's what California sellers typically experience:

Etsy is fastest for initial takedowns (1–3 days) and most willing to permanently ban repeat infringers. It's the best platform for handmade and creative sellers dealing with copycat designs.

eBay's VeRO program is the most robust system overall, with fast response times (24–48 hours) and strong support for brand owners — but requires upfront enrollment.

Shopify is slowest and most favorable to store owners, since it follows the strict DMCA statutory timeline and generally won't shut down entire stores. Cease and desist letters are almost always necessary for Shopify disputes.

For all three platforms, the most effective strategy is the same: file the platform complaint for immediate relief, then send a [cease and desist letter](/blog/2026-05-13-cease-and-desist-letters-ecommerce-sellers-counterfeit-goods) to the infringer directly for lasting resolution.
