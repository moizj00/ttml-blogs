# A Medical Bill You Don't Owe Just Hit Collections. Here's Your Move.

The envelope doesn't say "hospital" anymore. It says a collection agency you've never heard of, for a number you don't recognize, tied to a visit your insurance was supposed to cover.

First, breathe. Then know this: a bill in collections is a claim, not a verdict. You have a specific, time-sensitive right to make them prove it.

## Make them prove it, in writing, within 30 days

Federal law hands you a debt-validation right. When a collector first contacts you, you have 30 days to demand in writing that they verify the debt — who they say you owe, how much, and the paperwork behind it (the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1692g). Send that letter, and they have to pause collection until they produce it.

A surprising number of medical "debts" don't survive that step. Codes get duplicated. Insurance gets billed wrong. Charges land on the wrong patient. The validation letter is the cheapest, fastest filter there is.

## California stacks more on top

California's Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (Civil Code § 1788 and following) pulls medical-bill collectors under strict rules and bars the harassing tactics — the repeat calls, the threats, the we'll-call-your-employer routine.

The state has also moved hard on medical debt and your credit: recent California law sharply limits whether unpaid medical bills can land on your credit report at all. The mechanics are technical and still settling, so this is a place to get current advice rather than assume. The direction, though, is in your favor.

(Debt and credit-reporting law is technical, federal and state rules interact, and timing matters. Confirm the specifics with a licensed attorney before you rely on any of it.)

## What the letter does that a phone call can't

Call the collector and you get a script and a denial you can't prove later. Send a letter and you create a dated record, trigger your legal rights, and put the burden back where it belongs: on them, to show the debt is real and yours.

Keep it factual. State that you dispute the debt, demand validation, and note the date. Send it a way you can prove it arrived.

If the collector ignores a proper dispute, keeps reporting a bill you don't owe, or crosses into harassment, a letter on a law firm's letterhead changes their math fast. That's what we do — a flat fee, the right letter, sent now.

A bill you don't owe isn't yours to carry quietly. Make them prove it, in writing, and let the law do the heavy part.
