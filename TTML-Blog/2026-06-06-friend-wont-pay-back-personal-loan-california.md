Yes. A loan to a friend or family member is a legally enforceable debt in California, even if nothing was signed. An attorney demand letter — a formal letter on law firm letterhead demanding repayment by a deadline — is usually the fastest, cheapest way to collect it. You have four years to act on a written loan agreement (California Code of Civil Procedure § 337) and two years on a verbal one (CCP § 339), so the debt does not disappear just because the loan was informal.

**Short answer:** Personal loans between friends are enforceable in California. A lawyer's demand letter often gets repayment started within weeks, costs a flat fee instead of a retainer, and preserves your right to sue in small claims court for up to $12,500 if the letter is ignored.

## Is a Loan to a Friend Legally Enforceable Without a Contract?

Yes. California recognizes oral contracts. If you gave money with a mutual understanding it would be paid back — not as a gift — a contract exists. The practical problem is proof, not validity. Text messages saying "I'll pay you back next month," Venmo or Zelle notes labeled "loan," partial repayments, and witnesses all establish that both sides treated the transfer as a loan.

The distinction that matters is gift versus loan. If your friend claims the money was a gift, the question becomes what the evidence shows at the time of transfer. This is why you should gather every message about the money before sending any demand.

## What Deadline Applies to Collecting a Personal Loan in California?

Two statutes control. For a written agreement — which can be as informal as a signed IOU or an email exchange confirming terms — you have four years from the breach under CCP § 337. For a purely verbal loan, you have two years under CCP § 339. The clock generally starts when repayment was due and not made, or from the last partial payment.

If the deadline is approaching, a demand letter should go out immediately — it doesn't pause the clock, but it frequently produces repayment or a written acknowledgment before you ever need to file.

## Why Does a Lawyer's Letter Work Better Than Asking Again Yourself?

Three reasons. First, it converts a personal favor into a legal obligation — your friend now knows an attorney has reviewed the claim and considers it collectible. Second, it sets a concrete deadline and consequence: pay by a stated date or face a small claims filing. Third, it gives your friend a face-saving exit. Many people pay after one attorney letter precisely because it lets them resolve the matter privately, before a court record exists.

A letter is also dramatically cheaper than litigation. A flat-fee attorney letter costs a fraction of what a collections lawsuit would, and you keep the entire amount recovered — unlike a collections agency, which typically keeps 25–50%.

## What Should the Demand Letter Include?

A strong personal-loan demand letter states the amount, the date and method of the loan (bank transfer, Venmo, cash), the repayment terms as both sides understood them, the evidence you hold, a payment deadline (typically 10–14 days), and the specific next step if ignored — usually a small claims filing. It should stay factual and unemotional. ## What If the Letter Is Ignored?

Small claims court is the natural next step for loans up to $12,500 (the limit for individuals under CCP § 116.221). You cannot bring a lawyer to argue for you in California small claims, but the demand letter itself becomes evidence — it shows the judge you made a good-faith effort to resolve the debt, and many courts expect a pre-filing demand. For amounts above the limit, you can waive the excess and still use small claims, or file a limited civil case.

## Will This Destroy the Friendship?

It may test it — but an unpaid loan already has. A professional letter is often less damaging than months of personal nagging, because it moves the dispute onto neutral, factual ground. Many borrowers respond with a payment plan, which a lawyer can document in a short written agreement so the new promise is enforceable for a fresh four-year period under CCP § 337.
