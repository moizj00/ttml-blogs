The ™ symbol signals that you claim rights in a name or logo as a trademark, whether or not it's registered — you can use it freely in California the moment you start using your brand. The ® symbol means the mark is federally registered with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office under the Lanham Act (15 U.S.C. § 1051), and you may only use it after registration is granted. Using ® before approval is improper and can jeopardize your rights.

**Short answer:** Use ™ for any unregistered brand name or logo you're claiming. Use ® only after the USPTO has actually registered the mark. Never use ® on a pending or unregistered mark — false claims of registration can be raised against you.

## When can you use the ™ symbol?

Anytime. The ™ (or ℠ for services) requires no application or approval. It puts the public on notice that you treat the word or design as your trademark and helps build the common-law rights California recognizes through actual use in commerce. Many businesses use ™ while a federal application is pending.

## When are you allowed to use the ® symbol?

Only once the USPTO issues a registration certificate for that specific mark, and only for the goods or services covered by the registration. A California state registration through the Secretary of State does not entitle you to the federal ® symbol. Using ® on an unregistered or still-pending mark can be treated as a misrepresentation and may bar you from recovering certain remedies in an infringement case.

## Does the symbol affect your legal protection?

Proper notice matters. Using ® on a registered mark can strengthen your ability to recover damages and profits, because it provides constructive notice of your rights. ## What happens if you use the ® symbol by mistake?

Improperly using the registered-trademark symbol — on a mark that's only pending, only registered at the state level, or not registered at all — can have real consequences. If the misuse was deliberate and you then try to enforce the mark, a court can deny you certain remedies, because you misrepresented your rights to the public. Even an innocent mistake is worth correcting quickly: switch to ™ until your federal registration actually issues.

The practical rule is simple. While your application is pending, use ™. The day the USPTO issues your registration certificate, you may switch to ® for the goods and services it covers. If you sell different product lines, only use ® on the ones actually covered by a registration.

## Do you have to use any symbol at all?

No. Using ™ or ® is optional, not mandatory, and your underlying rights exist whether or not you display a symbol. But proper notice has practical value, especially the ®, because it provides constructive notice of your federal registration and can support a stronger damages claim if someone infringes. For most California brands, the habit worth building is: use ™ from day one, and upgrade to ® the moment you're entitled to.
