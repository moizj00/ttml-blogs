# Genre: Creative & Narrative

Fiction, storytelling, scripts, personal/memoir. Where "human-like sentiment" is the whole game.

## The job
Make the reader *feel* something true, through experience rather than explanation. Story is the smuggling of emotion inside event.

## Show, don't tell — operationalized
- Replace emotion-labels with their physical evidence. Not "she was nervous" → "she kept checking a phone that hadn't buzzed."
- Trust the reader. State less than you want to. The gap is where feeling happens.
- Detail is character. *Which* objects a person notices tells us who they are.

## Scene craft
- **Start in the middle of motion.** Drop the reader into a moment, not a setup. Context can arrive later, on the move.
- **Concrete sensory anchors.** One or two precise sensory details ground a scene better than a paragraph of description. Specific, not exhaustive.
- **Dialogue carries subtext.** People rarely say what they mean. The tension lives between the lines. Cut "he said angrily" — let the line be angry.
- **Vary the camera.** Wide, then close. A long descriptive sentence, then a two-word reaction.

## Voice & sentiment
- Restraint amplifies. The saddest moments are often told flat. Overwrought language deflates emotion; understatement detonates it.
- A single specific image can do what a paragraph of feeling-words can't. Find the one detail that holds the whole emotion.
- Earn sentiment — don't reach for it. Manipulation (sad music, on-the-nose lines) reads as cheap. Let the reader arrive at the feeling themselves.

## Rhythm & prose
- Sentence length is your heartbeat. Slow it down for weight, speed it up for panic.
- Cut adverbs and adjective-stacks ruthlessly here especially. The noun and verb should carry it.
- Read every line aloud. Prose rhythm is even more audible in narrative.

## Originality & safety
- Create original characters, worlds, and plots. Don't reproduce copyrighted characters or named real public figures in invented scenes.

## Do
- Open mid-motion. Show through detail. Trust the gap. Understate the climax.

## Don't
- Don't announce feelings. Don't over-describe. Don't tie a moral bow on the ending.
