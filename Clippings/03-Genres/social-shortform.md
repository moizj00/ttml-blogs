# Genre: Social & Short-Form

X/Twitter, LinkedIn, captions, hooks. Constraint is the medium. Every word fights for its place.

## The job
Stop the scroll, earn the read, leave one clean idea behind. You have one breath.

## Universal short-form rules
- **First line is 90% of the work.** It must create a gap the reader needs closed — curiosity, tension, a stake, a contrarian claim.
- **One idea per post.** If you have two, you have two posts.
- **Front-load.** No wind-up. The payoff or the promise comes first; the explanation follows.
- **Concrete and personal beats abstract and clever.** A real number, a real moment, a real opinion.
- **Cut the last line if it's a wrap-up.** Short-form rarely needs a conclusion.

## Hooks that work
- A specific number or result: "Spent 6 years doing X. Here's what I'd skip."
- A reversal: "Everyone says X. They're wrong, and it's costing you."
- A confession: "I almost killed the project the week before it worked."
- A sharp question that isn't rhetorical filler.

## Platform notes
- **X/Twitter:** terse, lowercase-friendly if that's your voice, punchy. Threads = one idea per tweet, each able to stand alone. No "a thread 🧵" throat-clearing unless it's genuinely your style.
- **LinkedIn:** still write like a human. Avoid the "broetry" one-line-per-paragraph cliché and the humble-brag "I'm thrilled to announce" template. A real story with a real lesson outperforms the formula. Short paragraphs are fine; emptiness dressed as profundity is not.
- **Captions:** match the image, don't describe it. Add the thing the picture can't say.

## Anti-tells specific to social
- No "Let that sink in." No "Read that again." No "Unpopular opinion:" (it's always a popular opinion).
- No hashtag walls. A few, relevant, or none.
- No fake vulnerability for engagement. Readers smell it.
- Emoji only if it's authentically your voice, and then sparingly.

## Do
- Lead with the sharpest line. Keep one idea. Stop early.

## Don't
- Don't bury the point in paragraph three. There is no paragraph three.
