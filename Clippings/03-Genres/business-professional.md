# Genre: Business & Professional

Emails, proposals, reports, internal comms, sales/marketing copy. Clarity under time pressure.

## The job
Get a busy person to understand and act, fast, without making them feel managed by a robot. Professional ≠ stiff. The best business writing is plain, warm, and specific.

## Universal rules
- **Bottom line up front (BLUF).** The ask, the decision, or the headline goes in the first line or two. Don't make the reader hunt.
- **Plain English.** Kill the corporate dialect: "circle back," "leverage," "synergies," "bandwidth," "low-hanging fruit," "move the needle," "boil the ocean," "touch base." Say the real thing.
- **Respect their time.** Shorter is kinder. If it can be three sentences, don't make it three paragraphs.
- **One purpose per message.** Mixed asks get half-answered.
- **Make the next step obvious.** What do you want them to do, by when?

## Emails
- **Subject line = the actual content,** not "Quick question" or "Touching base." E.g. "Approve Q3 budget by Fri?" 
- Open with the point, not "I hope this email finds you well."
- Warm but efficient. A human sentence ("Thanks for jumping on this") beats robotic courtesy.
- End with a clear, single call to action.

## Proposals & reports
- Lead with the recommendation and the why. Detail and methodology support it underneath — they don't precede it.
- Use structure (headers, the occasional table or list) because here scanning genuinely helps. But the prose between still has to be readable, not bullet-soup.
- Quantify. "Cuts onboarding from 3 weeks to 4 days" beats "significantly improves onboarding."
- Name risks honestly. It builds trust and preempts objections.

## Sales & marketing copy
- Sell the outcome, not the feature. The reader cares what changes for them.
- Specific proof over adjectives. A number, a customer, a result — not "world-class" or "best-in-class."
- One clear CTA. Don't dilute with five links.
- Avoid hype words (see [[anti-ai-tells]] section B). Hype reads as insecurity.

## Tone calibration
- Match the relationship and the stakes. A note to a peer ≠ a note to a board.
- Default warm-direct. Soften only when delivering bad news — and then be clear *and* kind, never mealy.

## Do
- State the point first. Use plain words. Make the ask explicit.

## Don't
- Don't bury the lede under pleasantries. Don't speak corporate. Don't pad to seem thorough.
