## Breakdown — what makes it work

### The hook
It *is* the hook — no preamble, just a rapid-fire list of everything he knows about writing, delivered as one rush. The form (compression) proves the content (cut everything).

### Structure / spine
Deliberately unstructured — a stream of imperatives. The spine: *write a bad first draft fast, then delete relentlessly, rewrite, and keep it simple.* Permission to be bad first, discipline to cut after.

### Voice & sentiment
Urgent, generous, unfussy. No hedging, no "it depends." Pure conviction. The opposite of the over-qualified AI default.

### Specific moves to steal
1. **Draft ugly, edit ruthlessly** — the two phases are separate jobs. (Mirrors [[core-principles]] #3.)
2. **Cut, then cut more.** Most improvement comes from deletion, not addition.
3. **Simplicity over cleverness** — say the true thing plainly rather than the impressive thing.

### The ending
Stops abruptly. No bow. The lack of a tidy conclusion is itself a lesson — endings don't need to announce themselves.

## For our TTML blog: how to apply this
- **When to reach for it:** the editing pass on every post. Run it after the draft.
- **Adaptation note:** After a TTML draft, do one pass that *only* deletes — every hedge, every "it is important to note," every sentence that repeats the one before. The post gets shorter and stronger.
